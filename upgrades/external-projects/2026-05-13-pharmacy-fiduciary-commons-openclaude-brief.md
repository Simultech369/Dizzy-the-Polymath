## Review Goal

Evaluate whether the project is aligned with Dizzy's anti-extractive, continuity-and-judgment, fiduciary, local-first, trust-zone-aware worldview.

Do not force a yes/no answer.

Find:

- genuine alignment
- points of contention
- unresolved doctrine questions
- business-model and governance risks
- places where Dizzy's philosophy may need refinement

## Core Comparison Questions

### Anti-Extraction

- Does the project reduce chokepoint power or merely move it?
- Does it expose hidden flows in a way weaker participants can act on?
- Does it depend on the persistence of rebate opacity or complexity?
- Does monetization scale with transparency and savings, or with continued confusion?

### Fiduciary Design

- Who is the beneficiary?
- Who is the buyer?
- Who controls data and pricing logic?
- What fiduciary duty is claimed, and how is it enforced?
- Is the project merely advisory, or does it create enforceable accountability?

### Freedom And Capability

- Who gains exit power?
- Who gains bargaining power?
- Who becomes more dependent?
- What material capability does the project give patients, employers, plan sponsors, pharmacists, or public institutions?

### Governance

- What must users trust the operator to do correctly?
- What is auditable?
- What is contestable?
- Who interprets disputes?
- What happens if the operator sells, pivots, disappears, or is captured?

### Security And Privacy

- What sensitive data is involved?
- What access-control and data-leakage risks are real?
- Are there prompt-injection or indirect-input risks if AI is used?
- What should be transparent versus private?

1. alignment rating with confidence

## Key Skeptical Prompt

Ask whether the project is anti-extractive in outcome or merely anti-extractive in rhetoric.

A project can expose PBM rebates and still become dependent on the same rebate complexity it criticizes.

OpenClaude should identify whether project success requires simplifying the system over time, or whether the project profits most when the system remains confusing.
