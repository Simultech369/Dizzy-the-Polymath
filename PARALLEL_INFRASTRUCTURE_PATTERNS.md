# Pattern Library for Lawful Parallel Commons Infrastructure

This document outlines the pattern library for parallel, commons-based, well-being-first infrastructure. It defines adjacent primitives for health access, as well as extensions to other foundational sectors (housing, energy, data, credentials, food).

---

## 1. Parallel Health Infrastructure Primitives

These primitives establish a lawful, parallel infrastructure for independent pharmacy resilience and patient protection, separate from corporate intermediary capture.

### P-01: Rebate Transparency Registry
- **Problem**: Pharmacy Benefit Managers (PBMs) operate in opacity, concealing actual rebate flow volumes, clawbacks, and net pricing gaps.
- **Mechanism**: A public, legally careful ledger displaying expected, recorded, and non-recorded rebate flows. Inputs are derived from audited dispensing logs and actual treasury payouts.
- **Purpose**: Make chokepoint opacity expensive and legally contestable through public, factual evidence of omission gaps.

### P-02: Independent Pharmacy Cooperative Procurement Layer
- **Problem**: Single independent pharmacies face severe purchasing and pricing asymmetries when negotiating with massive wholesale distributors and PBMs.
- **Mechanism**: A federated, cooperative purchasing and formulary intelligence system. Enables shared vendor negotiation, automated price-dispersion analysis, and real-time formulary exclusion detection.
- **Purpose**: Rebalance negotiating power and reduce buyer-side asymmetries.

### P-03: Patient Assistance Commons
- **Problem**: Hardship programs are typically controlled by drug manufacturers (copay cards) or centralized charities, which use them for marketing or tax optimization and can revoke access arbitrarily.
- **Mechanism**: A nonprofit or cooperative-governed fund that directs rebate surplus directly into medication access grants, transportation subsidies, and emergency fills.
- **Purpose**: Guarantee a basic access floor governed by the community, not the producer.

### P-04: Health Access Participatory Budgeting & Quadratic Funding
- **Problem**: Top-down allocation of charitable health funds fails to target the actual, granular needs of local patients and independent pharmacies.
- **Mechanism**: Epoch-based matching rounds where community projects are proposed, and pharmacy/patient votes determine matching ratios from the rebate treasury's `patientFund`.
- **Purpose**: Direct matching surplus to projects defined and vetted by the people carrying the system's operational burden.

### P-05: Pharmacy Mutual Credit / Voucher Ledger
- **Problem**: Independent pharmacies experience extreme liquidity squeezes due to delayed PBM reimbursements and clawbacks, risking closure.
- **Mechanism**: A clearing and voucher system separate from the core rebate treasury. Supports interest-free emergency medication credit, community health vouchers, and inter-pharmacy credit lines.
- **Purpose**: Provide local bridge support and mutual clearing without relying on extractive bank debt.

### P-06: Open Formulary & Pricing Intelligence
- **Problem**: PBMs manipulate formulary exclusions and clawback schedules to force patients onto higher-cost drugs and squeeze independent margins.
- **Mechanism**: Lawful data analytics tracking reimbursement gaps, price dispersion, clawback patterns, and pharmacy closures.
- **Purpose**: Render market extraction visible to regulators, patients, and cooperatives.

### P-07: Verifiable Credential Layer
- **Problem**: Centralized identity and licensing platforms create gatekeeper risk and platform lock-in for independent pharmacists and patient advocates.
- **Mechanism**: Decentralized credentials for independent pharmacies, patient advocates, auditors, and council members. Built on portable, open-standards cryptography (e.g. W3C VC).
- **Purpose**: Prevent platform capture and ensure credential portability across jurisdictions.

### P-08: Community Audit Boards
- **Problem**: Technical smart contract logs and complex accounting sheets are illegible to the community, hiding favoritism or administrative creep.
- **Mechanism**: Independent, plain-language audit boards that review fund flows, omissions, conflicts of interest, and outcomes.
- **Purpose**: Ensure institutional legitimacy and accountability without vigilantism.

### P-09: Anti-Hoarding Surplus Circulation Policy
- **Problem**: Commons funds can easily pool and stagnate under defensive governance, failing to serve active needs.
- **Mechanism**: A rule requiring that idle treasury funds beyond a specific duration are either allocated to reserves with explicit, documented purposes, or automatically swept into the next PB/QF round.
- **Purpose**: Ensure surplus actively circulates toward preventative access.

### P-10: Exit & Portability Standards
- **Problem**: Data and asset lock-in turns cooperative platforms into new extractive chokepoints.
- **Mechanism**: Standards ensuring that every participant (pharmacy or patient) can export their full claims history, credentials, receipts, and voting records at any time.
- **Purpose**: Protect exit rights as the ultimate defense against institutional capture.

## 2. Surplus Routing in Other Primitives (Housing, Energy, Data, Food)

The same commons-based surplus routing and preventative floor primitives apply to other foundational sectors, always centering on portability and anti-lock-in.

### 2.1 Housing Trusts
- **Primitive**: Land and housing equity commons.
- **Surplus Routing**: Ground rent or equity gains are routed into a local maintenance and acquisition fund.
- **Portability & Exit**: Residents retain portable equity credits or tenancy records, ensuring they can move to other trust-cooperatives without losing their accumulated contribution value.

### 2.2 Energy Commons
- **Primitive**: Local microgrids and cooperative generation.
- **Surplus Routing**: Profit from grid feed-ins is routed into grid maintenance, battery upgrades, and energy assistance grants for low-income residents.
- **Portability & Exit**: Grid usage, billing history, and solar contribution tokens must be exportable, allowing users to exit to other microgrids or public grids without penalty.

### 2.3 Data & Computation Commons
- **Primitive**: Distributed compute networks and cooperative databases.
- **Surplus Routing**: Compute surplus or database subscription fees are routed into hardware upgrades, developer grants, and open-source tooling.
- **Portability & Exit**: Strict data portability. Users retain full rights to export their files, model weights, and history, with simple, verifiable revocation keys to delete their data from the commons.

### 2.4 Food & Agricultural Commons
- **Primitive**: Community-supported agriculture (CSA) and distribution hubs.
- **Surplus Routing**: Wholesale savings and excess produce are routed into local food pantries, farmer equipment loans, and soil-regeneration projects.
- **Portability & Exit**: Farmers and consumers retain portable records of subscription histories, supply credentials, and cooperative voting rights.
